# Model notes

## Design rule: one universe, nested opportunities

The simulator does not treat the seven epochs as mutually exclusive modes of nature. Epoch labels control the camera and dominant explanatory layer, while the state variables remain causally linked across the whole run.

## Core state variables

- `gradient`: available departure from equilibrium.
- `entropy`: monotonically increasing global disorder proxy.
- `complexity`: local realized structural/informational complexity proxy.
- `erd`: energy-rate density of the currently emphasized system.
- `heavies`: heavy-element enrichment generated by stellar evolution.
- `temperate`: fraction of planetary environments within a useful energy window.

## Threshold logic

Each universe derives hidden traits from a deterministic seed. Thresholds are therefore repeatable for a given seed but vary between universes.

The principal thresholds are deliberately asymmetric:

- Early structure needs fluctuations strong enough to survive dissipation.
- Stars need persistent galactic structure and dense gas.
- Planets need heavy-element enrichment plus a disk that survives the stellar environment.
- Complex chemistry needs raw materials and an energy window that is neither too weak nor too destructive.
- Life needs persistent chemical complexity and continuous throughput.
- Intelligence and culture require robust living ecosystems but remain contingent.

## Selection

Selection is modeled as environmental filtering, not as an agent choosing winners. Candidate structures that cannot retain enough energy throughput, stability, or environmental fit fail to become persistent structures.

## Energy-rate density scale

The UI reports energy-rate density relative to a typical star (`1×`) and plots it logarithmically. Representative target orders of magnitude are:

- star: ~1×
- planetary climate: ~100×
- plant-scale metabolism: ~1,000×
- high biological / neural processing: much higher
- technological civilization: approaching ~1,000,000×

These values are used as conceptual visual anchors, not as a dataset for precision numerical work.

## Why the timeline has equal-width epochs

Physical epoch durations differ by orders of magnitude. A literal linear 14-billion-year slider would make the Chemical and especially Cultural epochs almost impossible to interact with. The UI therefore allocates equal screen width to all seven epochs and maps each band to its own cosmic-age interval.
