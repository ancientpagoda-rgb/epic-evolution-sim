# Scientific model notes

## What the seven epochs mean

The seven epoch names come from Eric Chaisson's *Epic of Evolution: Seven Ages of the Cosmos*. They are used as explanatory camera layers, not mutually exclusive intervals. Real processes overlap: galaxy assembly continues while stars form; stars continue to evolve while planets form; chemistry, biology, and culture all remain embedded in older physical systems.

## Current-science timeline anchors

Version 2 updates the visual timeline to modern reference values while preserving the book's conceptual sequence:

- Universe age: about **13.8 billion years**.
- Recombination / release of the cosmic microwave background: about **380,000 years** after the Big Bang.
- First stars: plausibly as early as roughly **100–200 million years** after the Big Bang; exact details remain under active study.
- Solar System formation: about **4.6 billion years ago**.
- Robust evidence for terrestrial life: **well over 3.5 billion years ago**; older candidate evidence is less certain.
- Great Oxidation Event: about **2.4 billion years ago**.
- Human evolutionary lineage: roughly **six million years**; Homo sapiens about **300,000 years**; agriculture within roughly **12,000 years**.

The Particle Epoch uses a logarithmic age mapping because no linear display can make sub-second physics and a 380,000-year recombination time simultaneously visible. Other bands use compressed nonlinear display time for interaction. The screen is therefore not a literal linear clock.

## Updated element production

The first version inherited an older simplified narrative in which supernovae did nearly all heavy-element production. Version 2 separates several channels. Stellar fusion makes many nuclei through iron; winds and late stellar evolution recycle matter; explosive events distribute additional nuclei; neutron-star mergers and possibly other rare neutron-rich events contribute to r-process elements such as gold and platinum. The exact inventory by source remains an active research topic.

## Origin of life: uncertainty is part of the model

The simulation does not claim that a specific primordial-soup, hydrothermal-vent, RNA-first, metabolism-first, or exogenous pathway is established history. It displays several chemically plausible energy sources and lets persistent compartmentalized networks emerge only as a stochastic model threshold. Crossing that threshold is explicitly labeled a modeled outcome.

## Core state variables

- `gradient`: available departure from equilibrium.
- `entropy`: monotonically increasing global disorder proxy.
- `complexity`: local realized structural/informational complexity proxy.
- `erd`: Chaisson-style energy-rate density of the currently emphasized system.
- `heavies`: chemical enrichment generated by stellar evolution.
- `water`: availability of persistent water-bearing rocky environments in the selected planetary pathway.
- `temperate`: a broad useful-energy-window proxy, not a classical habitable-zone calculator.

## Selection and contingency

Selection is environmental filtering, not an agent choosing winners. Candidate structures fail when they cannot retain adequate stability, resources, or energy throughput. Biological evolution additionally includes inherited variation; the model description explicitly acknowledges genetic drift and symbiosis alongside natural selection.

## Energy-rate density

The normalized energy-rate-density display is retained because it is central to Chaisson's framework. The UI now labels it **Chaisson-style** and **conceptual**. It should not be read as a universally accepted one-dimensional definition of complexity or as a precision observational dataset.

## Rendering and transitions

Ultra mode targets roughly a 4K-UHD pixel budget (~8.9 megapixels), capped at 4× CSS resolution. High-DPI rendering is real framebuffer supersampling, not CSS enlargement. Scene transitions overlap over the final ~30% of each display band, with shared visual anchors intended to imply a continuous zoom through scale rather than a sequence of unrelated slides.

## Reference baseline used for Version 2

The implementation was checked against current public scientific material from NASA Science, ESA/Planck, NASA Astrobiology, the Smithsonian National Museum of Natural History, and the Smithsonian Human Origins Program. These references update numerical chronology and areas where the 2000s-era book necessarily predates later evidence. The book remains the conceptual basis for the seven-epoch structure and energy/complexity framing.
